# white_monster
baddie

A one-page 3D site. A 3D-scanned White Monster can spins on a hologram
projector. Press **unleash the baddie** and it charges up, dissolves into a
swarm of light, and re-forms as a goth baddie. Press **back in the can** to put
her back.

She's a realistic, full-size avatar in the White Monster palette (white,
black, silver): long platinum hair with curtain bangs, pointed elf ears and
glossy black horns, winged liner, a plum-black lip with snake bites, a spiked
choker with an O-ring, a cropped white knit sweater with a black claw print, a
black pleated mini with a studded belt and hip chains, fishnets, spiked thigh
garters, striped thigh-highs and platform boots. She stands with a hand on her
hip, holds the can up by her chin, breathes, follows your cursor with her head
and eyes, and spins when you tap her.

## Run it

It's a static site with no build step. Serve the folder with any static file
server:

```sh
npx serve .
# or
python3 -m http.server
```

Then open the printed URL. Opening `index.html` directly from disk won't work,
because ES modules need to be served over http. GitHub Pages works as is.

Three.js (r186) comes from jsDelivr via an import map, and the fonts come from
Google Fonts. The models live in `models/`. The projector's textures are
painted on a `<canvas>` at load time, and the sound effects are synthesized
with Web Audio.

The page shows the can as soon as it loads (about 2 MB, or 3.5 MB on desktop,
where it uses a 4K texture) and streams her in (about 3.5 MB) while you look
at it. If you press the button before she has arrived, the can keeps charging
until she has.

## Controls

- **drag**: orbit
- **scroll / pinch**: zoom
- **tap the can**: it does a barrel roll
- **tap her**: she spins and the caption changes
- **sound on/off**: top-right chip, remembered per browser

Two URL flags help with recording and screenshots:

- `?mode=baddie`: start with her already out
- `?dt=0.05`: advance the animation by a fixed step per frame

## How it's built

| file | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| `js/main.js` | renderer, lights, bloom, camera framing, UI, loading, and the transform timeline |
| `js/can.js` | loads the scanned can; one geometry serves the projector can and the one in her hand |
| `js/avatar.js` | loads her, sets up materials, poses her (two-bone IK for the arms, finger curls), idle motion |
| `js/textures.js` | canvas painters for the projector's sigil and the floor shadow |
| `js/reveal.js` | shader patch behind the "hologram print" dissolve and the glowing seam |
| `js/particles.js` | surface-sampled particle swarm (skinned meshes are sampled in their current pose) |
| `js/stage.js` | projector pedestal, light beam, embers, floor and sky |
| `js/audio.js` | can crack, riser, whoosh, boom and sparkle sounds |
| `models/can/` | the can scan as a web GLB plus 4K and 2K textures |
| `models/avatar/` | her rigged GLB and textures |
| `tools/can/` | converts the Sketchfab download into `models/can/` |
| `tools/avatar/` | builds `models/avatar/` from MakeHuman's CC0 assets ([details](tools/avatar/README.md)) |

The transform is one shared timeline. A seam sweeps down through the source
object and discards everything above it. Particles leave each point on the
surface as the seam passes it and spiral around the projector. They land on
the target just as its own seam sweeps up from the floor, so the swarm and
the reveal stay in sync.

### Rebuilding the models

Both are generated by Python scripts (numpy and Pillow):

```sh
# the can, from Sketchfab's glTF download of the scan (folder or .zip)
python3 tools/can/convert.py ~/Downloads/white_monster_3d_scan.zip

# her: fetch MakeHuman's assets once, then build
tools/avatar/fetch_makehuman.sh
python3 tools/avatar/build.py
```
